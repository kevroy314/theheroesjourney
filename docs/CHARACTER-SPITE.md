# Spite: the locked design

Issue #3 asks for one thing before any more character art is made: **a design
that is decided, written down, and reproducible.** This is that document. The
art it describes is authored in `tools/make_sprites.py` and regenerated by
`python3 tools/make_sprites.py`.

Files:

| what | path | size |
| --- | --- | --- |
| dialogue portrait, alter `kind` | `assets/portraits/spite_kind.png` | 112 x 112 |
| dialogue portrait, alter `wary` | `assets/portraits/spite_wary.png` | 112 x 112 |
| overworld sprite sheet | `assets/sprites/spite.png` | 3 cols x 4 rows of 32 x 48 |

The portrait paths are not a choice. `Dialogue.portrait_path()` resolves
`res://assets/portraits/<stem>_<mood>.png`, the stem comes from `portrait` on the
speaker in `data/dialogue/spite.json`, and the moods are the two alter ids in
`data/content/spite.json`. Renaming any of those three things breaks the art
without breaking the build.

---

## 1. The reading

The writing came first and it is unusually specific, so the drawing's only real
job was to not contradict it.

> "Because I am what is left over when you talk yourself out of things. There is
> a great deal of me. I would like there to be less."

Three things follow from that line and they are the whole brief.

**He is not a villain.** A hooded menace or a grinning demon would be a
different character. So would something cute. He is the sneer, and the sneer is
ashamed of itself.

**He wants to be smaller.** So he is thin, hunched, and — this is the one
compositional decision in the portrait — he is placed *small and low in his own
frame*, with eleven of the plate's fifty-six rows left empty above his head. A
character who says that line does not get a heroic bust that fills its plate.

**He has been sitting while you walked.** "Four hundred and six. That's how many
times you have opened that door." He carries nothing: no pack, no bedroll, no
belt kit. The traveller is equipped for a journey; Spite is not going anywhere.

## 2. The three inversions

The reason to draw a second character is to say something the first one cannot.
Everything specific about Spite is stated against the player.

**No hood.** The traveller is a hood with a suggestion of a face inside it — you
have played four hundred runs without seeing who you are. Spite is a bare head
and a whole face. The crowns differ, which is the first thing the eye reads on a
32-pixel figure, and it means the first face in the game belongs to the part of
you that sneers.

**Arms in.** Every frame of the traveller has arms swinging *outside* his own
outline; `make_sprites.py` spends a paragraph on why. Spite walks with his hands
in his pockets and his elbows out, arms inside the silhouette. Open against
closed, said in the outline rather than in the face, so it survives the zoom.

**The scarf, untied.** He wears the same object the traveller wears: the one
saturated garment, in the same `WARM` ramp, in the same place on the body. The
traveller has it wrapped twice at the throat. Spite has it hanging loose in two
uneven ends. Same colour, same cloth, worn as though abandoned — the shortest
way to draw "I am part of you" without drawing your face.

That last point matters for a reason outside Spite. The Warden is already *"your
face, thirty years further on"*, so a second character built out of the player's
face would be the same trick twice. **Spite is not the player's face. He is the
player's coat.**

## 3. The two alters

`data/content/spite.json` names them and a `mood` on a dialogue node selects one,
so the doorstep conversation and the random encounters are one person.

- `kind` — *the one who remembers you.* Head level, eyes open, brows level,
  mouth level, shoulders down, collar down so the scarf shows.
- `wary` — *the one who remembers the endings.* Head sunk a row into shoulders
  raised two, upper lids down over the top row of each eye, one brow a row
  higher than the other, two pixels of mouth-corner lifted, collar up over the
  jaw.

**The light does not change between them.** Both halves are the same man in the
same doorway at the same hour. An early cut dimmed `wary` a step and bought a
corpse rather than a mood; every difference is now geometry, and 20% of the
plate's pixels differ between the two.

They are one geometry table plus a delta, so the skull, the hair, the nose and
the scarf cannot drift. That is the point — see below.

## 4. Authored, not generated. Zero quota spent.

The `game-art-pipeline` skill's hard finding is that the model cannot hold an
identity across images: sprite sheets come back as labelled contact sheets with
a redrawn character in every cell. The usual conclusion is "generate the
portrait, author the sprite", because a portrait is one image at one size.

**That does not hold here, because there are two portraits of one man.** `kind`
and `wary` is the identity-across-images problem with two cells instead of
twelve. Generating them separately buys two different men, and "the same person,
twice" is the entire requirement. Generating one and hand-editing the mood is
just authoring with an off-palette starting point.

The second argument is the cast. The traveller, the dog and the cat are all
authored in code from the ramps in `data/themes/firstlight.json`. A quantised
render does not land in those ramps, and a cast where one member is off-palette
reads as a cast with a guest in it.

So: **nothing was generated and no image quota was spent.** Every colour on him
is mixed from the theme — `HAIR`, `SSKIN`, and the traveller's own `COOL`,
`WARM` and `LEATHER`.

## 5. The numbers the art has to hit

Reported by `python3 tools/make_sprites.py`, measured against the real tileset
rather than asserted:

- 22 px across the shoulders against the traveller's 24, and 42 rows of figure
  against 44 — narrower, and hunched.
- Mean luma 70 against the traveller's 74, bracketed either side of every
  walkable ground: worst case `grass_tall`, where 55% of him reads light against
  it, 24% dark and 21% carries no read of its own.
- Same near-black 8-connected rim and same baked contact shadow as every other
  figure and standing prop, so he is lit by the same sun.
- Same sheet contract: column 0 is the neutral pose, the walk plays 1, 0, 2, 0,
  `right` is a mirror of `left`, the sole sits on a fixed row in every frame.

## 6. Check sheets

Look at these rather than at the PNGs:

- `assets/portraits/_spite_portraits.png` — both moods at 1:1 on the real panel
  colour, and again at 4x. The 1:1 half is the one that matters; a face that
  only works enlarged is not a portrait.
- `assets/sprites/_spite_x4.png` — the sheet with the cell grid ruled over it.
- `assets/sprites/_spite_ground.png` — the traveller and Spite standing side by
  side on all eighteen walkable materials, at the zoom the game runs at.
- `assets/sprites/_spite_walk.png` — the cycle in playback order.

## 7. Still open

**He is placed.** (This section used to say nothing placed him. That has been
true since the critter system learned about `sheet`.) `data/content/critters.json`
carries the species with its `{cols: 3, rows: 4, w: 32, h: 48}` override,
`data/content/interactables.json` has the `Talk` verb, and `data/world/overworld.json`
puts him on the step. The autoload that walks him is `Critters` — there is no
`HJCritters` symbol; it has no `class_name`.

What is actually still open:

- **He is the exception, not the pattern.** The town is authored on the shared
  base in `docs/CHARACTER-TOWNSFOLK.md` — one parameter dictionary per person —
  and Spite's hand-typed tables were deliberately left alone. That is the right
  call for a major character and the wrong one for the fifteenth townsperson, so
  do not copy this file's approach without reading that one first.
- **Nothing validates the mood → portrait link.** `Dialogue.portrait_path()`
  resolves `<stem>_<mood>.png` and nothing checks the file is there; rename an
  alter and the art silently stops resolving with no test failing. The schema
  now knows every speaker's mood ids (`speaker_moods`, added with the town), so
  this is one loop in `tools/validate_data.py`.
- **A critter is a state machine over `hold`, `approach`, `retreat` and
  `wander`.** Spite holds, which is all beat 6 needs. A character who has to do
  anything else needs the general entity plane (#34), not more art.
