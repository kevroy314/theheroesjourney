# The townsfolk: the shared base, and five people standing on it

`docs/CHARACTER-SPITE.md` is one character decided, written down and made
reproducible. This is the other half of that problem: **the second through
seventh characters, and the machinery that means the eighth is a dictionary
rather than a file.**

Spite is 330 lines of hand-typed geometry — eleven rows of skull, sixteen of
coat, a drawing function per garment. That is the right amount of work for the
second person in the game and exactly the wrong amount for the fifteenth, which
is what #77 asks for. The `character-creation` skill says so in its own
"what is not solved" section and names the answer: *a shared human base table
with a per-character delta.* This is that table.

Everything is authored in `tools/make_sprites.py` and regenerated by
`python3 tools/make_sprites.py`. **Zero image quota was spent, again**, for the
reasons §4 of the Spite doc gives and for one more that only shows up at this
scale: six people generated separately are six art styles.

## 1. What a townsperson is

```python
TOWNSPERSON = dict(
    crown=3, head_w=12, head_h=11, hairline=4,      # the skull
    neck_w=6, neck_rows=3,
    sh_w=18, chest_w=18, elbow_w=20,                # the width profile,
    waist_w=17, hem_w=16, hem_y=32,                 #   shoulder to hem
    eye_y=6, eye_sep=4, mouth_dy=3,                 # the face
    leg_w=4, leg_gap=2, stride=4,
    depth=0.86, lean=0,                             # the side view
    crown_kind="cap", garment="coat",
    shadow=9.5, props=(),
)
```

A character is that dictionary with a handful of keys changed, four ramps, and
zero to two small prop functions. Nineteen numbers, and the ones that matter are
about six of them.

Three functions turn the numbers into geometry, and they are the whole trick:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| `oval(top, rows, w)` | a skull, with two rows of taper at each end |
| `taper(y0, y1, keys)` | widths named at *fractions* of a run of rows, interpolated. A silhouette — broad and tapering, round and barrelled, narrow over a wide skirt — is six numbers in a list rather than sixteen typed spans |
| `paint_spans(f, spans, ramp, keys)` | `sculpt()` over an arbitrary ramp. Same north-west light, same one-step-lighter-left / one-step-darker-right. **This is the single function that makes the whole town look like it is standing in the same weather as the traveller**, and nothing below is allowed to paint a garment any other way |

Everything downstream of that — four facings, three columns, column 0 neutral,
the walk playing `1, 0, 2, 0`, `right` a mirror of `left`, one recentre offset
for all three profile poses, the alpha-derived rim, the baked contact shadow,
the sole on a fixed row — is shared in `hu_build_all()` and **cannot be got
wrong per character, because no character gets to write it.**

### The value discipline, generalised

`COOL` has a deliberate hole between luma 43 and 90 because sand (62), ice (66),
dune (68) and snow (72) all live there. That hole is not a property of blue; it
is a property of the ground. So:

```python
def cloth5(base):
    return [mix(base, C["text"], 0.34), base, mix(base, C["line"], 0.30),
            _toward_bg(base, 40.0), _toward_bg(base, 27.0)]
```

Three steps above 95 luma, two below 42, nothing in the middle — **for any hue**.
The two dark steps are *solved* for a target luma rather than mixed by a
fraction, because a fraction that brackets a pale linen apron puts a dark green
cloak straight into the sand. `hue5()` is the even ramp for the small parts —
hair, brows, a jar — which can afford to sit in the hole because the rim holds
the silhouette anyway.

### What is NOT shared

The player, the dog, the cat and Spite were **not** refactored onto this base.
That was deliberate: their outputs are byte-identical before and after this work
(proved by hash), Spite's tables are hand-tuned against four specific bugs, and
the payoff for moving him is zero. The skill's phrasing is the right one —
*Spite's approach becomes the exception for a major character rather than the
pattern for everyone.* The primitives are shared; the tables are not.

## 2. The cast, and the two axes they are separated on

The reason to draw a seventh character is still to say something the first six
cannot. At `ZOOM 3` on 32 px tiles, two things survive: **mass** and **crown**.
Colour is third and it is deliberately third, because on a phone at dusk it is
the first thing to go.

| id | mass | crown | garment | palette | what they are for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `kid_pell` | 39 rows, 18 wide | leaf crown, three uneven points | cloak | `LEAF` | the gate |
| `kid_bird` | **34 rows**, 16 wide | mop | cloak | `MOSS` | the counter |
| `busybody` | 40 rows, **24-wide hem under 16-wide shoulders** | bun | dress + shawl | `DRESS` / `ROSE` | errands, sweets, the cat |
| `bram` | 44 rows, **28 wide, barrel** | mop + full beard | layers + bundle | `SACK` + patches | trade, and a tragedy |
| `tobin` | 45 rows, **28 wide, wedge** | bald dome + beard | apron | `SLATE` / `LINEN` | the bar |
| `cobb` | 43 rows, **waist wider than chest** | horseshoe | waistcoat over shirtsleeves | `BRASS` / `LINEN` | the shop |

Eight crowns in the game now and no two the same: the traveller's hood, Spite's
parted cap, a leaf crown, a mop, a bun, a bald dome, a horseshoe. Bram and Tobin
are both 28 across and differ on taper — 28→24 is a barrel, 28→18 is a wedge —
which is a difference in the *outline* and therefore survives the zoom.

Three skin tones (`SKIN_PALE`, `SKIN_ASH`, `SKIN_WARM`, `SKIN_DEEP`) and four
hairs, paired so that **no face and hair are within 20 luma of each other**. The
first cut paired dark skin with dark hair on Bird and her head was one brown
lump; the fix was to swap the two children's hair, which also made them visual
opposites, which was free.

### The errors only looking caught

Every one of these got through the rules and was found by opening the contact
sheet. Each is now a comment at the line that fixes it.

| what it looked like | what it was | the general rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **a yellow bollard** | the legs defaulted to the garment ramp | trousers are not the coat. A figure needs a value break at the waist *and* a hue break at the hip |
| **mittens** on the whole cast | sleeves ended in the leather cuff ramp | hands are skin |
| **the bartender had no head** from behind | `crown_kind="bald"` drew no hair and the back view draws no face | a bald head still has a head from behind |
| **a wizard's hat** | the bun narrowed over three rows on top of the crown | three rows of taper on top of a head is a cone whatever you meant by it. A bun is a knob, set back |
| **a bib** | the shawl ran a wide V down the chest | the same failure Spite's scarf records. It sits *on* the shoulders and follows the arms down |
| **a cardboard box** | Bram's 26-wide torso in one ramp with a cross seam | the inner coat, lapped to one side. The asymmetry is the only thing in him that is not mirrored |
| **a plank** for an arm | a constant-width lit panel | two wide at the shoulder, four at the forearm. The taper *is* the elbow |
| **a startled stare** | spectacles as bright pixels outboard of each eye | draw the *bottom* of the lens, dark. A closed ring on a 12 px head is goggles |
| **a pole through a child's face** | the stick at the body's own edge, in profile | the head is drawn two proud of the shoulders and the nose one further. Three clear |
| **a second head** beside Bram's ear | the bundle taken up to the crown in profile | side-on it rides the shoulders and no higher |
| **a box over the head** from behind | the bundle drawn across the shoulders | there is no depth here. Two lobes, one either side, and the skull shows down the middle |
| **no brows** on the white-haired woman | brows drawn in `hair[3]` | use whichever of hair and skin is actually darker. A face with no brows at 32 px has no expression |
| **a pyramid** filling the lower plate | the portrait bust widened four px a row | six rows of yoke, then horizontal |
| **a totem pole** | nine rows of portrait neck | four. The same number Spite's had to come down to |

The generalised version of the beak check is worth stating on its own, because
the obvious form of it is wrong. Spite's rule reads as "skin darker than hair" —
but a dark-haired man has a lighter face and always will. The rule the failure
actually teaches is **the face must not be the brightest thing on the figure**,
and `town_report()` now measures that off the rendered cel: the hottest opaque
pixel must not be a skin colour. It passes for all six because somebody's apron,
sleeves, bun or cloak always outranks their nose.

Measured against the real tileset by `python3 tools/make_sprites.py`, which
raises `TOWNSFOLK CONTRACT FAILED` if any of them breaks.

  kid_bird    16 w  34 rows   481 px  luma  75.9   worst dune        lit 48% dark 35% lost 14%
  busybody    26 w  40 rows   666 px  luma  77.9   worst undergrowth lit 64% dark 24% lost 11%

Against the bar the Spite doc sets — "lost" under about a fifth on the worst
ground, light ≥ 45%, dark ≥ 24% on every one of the eighteen — the town clears
"lost" comfortably (worst 14%). Two of the six dip under 24% dark on their worst
ground (Bram 20%, Tobin 21%) and that is the cost of being the two pale-garment
characters; both are over 68% light there, so nothing is at risk. Every figure
sits inside `[0, 118, 255]` alpha, centres on 15.5 in all four facings, and puts
its sole on the same row in all three poses.

## 3. Who they are, and what each is *for*

### The children of the defence — `kid_pell`, `kid_bird` / speaker `fort`

`PROPOSALS/BUILDINGS.md` #15. The town is bounded by a river on two sides and
farm fences on the third and the only gap has a fort in it. **This is the
progression gate in NPC form.** You do not fight them; you are not allowed past
until the town is finished, and the thing that changes their mind is that the
town is finished.

Written as **children, not guards**. Their authority is invented, absolute, and
— the joke, and also the horror — correct. They have ranks. Pell made the rules
this morning. The rule binds *them* too, which is the whole design of the change
of heart: Pell takes the stick out of the gap because the jar is full and says
so, and would have kept it there forever otherwise. It is not a favour.

The refusal is real and it has three arguments against it, all of which fail,
and the third is the one that lands: *"we let one out before. Before the
counting. She said the same as you and she was nice about it and we moved and
she went up the lane."* That is you, four hundred and six times, from a child's
side of it.

Nobody in town knows whose children they are. Neither do they. One line, said
flat, never explained.

**Bird's jar is the objective's progress bar.** One pebble per hole she has
watched somebody close. Nothing in the dialogue counts anything of its own — see
§5 for how the condition is wired and what is still missing.

Two people, one speaker record, one conversation, **two critters** — Pell holds
the gap and Bird sits on the crate — because the fort has two children in it and
only one of them talks to you.

### Mrs Ollard — `busybody`

`PROPOSALS/NPC-CONCEPTS.md` #3, the most fully specified person in the notes and
therefore the one where the notes are the spec and the file is a transcription.
The comment at the top of `data/dialogue/busybody.json` tracks every clause of
Kevin's paragraph against what got built. The short version is in §6.

The one invention: **her secret name is `Sunny`**, it is what the cat quest pays
out, and the reason it is worth having is the line under it — *"it's what my
husband called me, and there's nobody left who's used it, and I have got very
tired of being Mrs Ollard at everybody."* The busybody is a comedy character
with a loneliness underneath, and the mechanic and the sadness are the same
object.

### Bram Hollis — `bram`

`NPC-CONCEPTS.md` #2. Six brothers, six twins, an empty house on the square that
is plainly theirs. **The brief said: do not tip the tragedy on the first
meeting**, so the first meeting is entirely warm — he is funny, he is delighted,
he gives you back something you dropped and undercharges for it, and the only
tell is that he counts in sixes and does it twice. The house gets one cheerful
deflection that is a beat too smooth. The whole story sits behind
`zone_gte: 2` — you have to have been out past the foothills and come back — and
even then he tells you the shape and not the thing.

Mechanically he is **a shop that runs backwards**, and the pricing rule from the
notes is live today: "he pays more for things from further out" is `zone_gte`,
which is a condition `Rules.passes` already implements, so the reply that pays
well is simply not on screen until you have been far enough to have anything
worth it.

### Tobin — `tobin`

The notes say `4. The bartender - he h` and stop, so he is invented. The tavern
is "seedy" and he had to be someone the player would come back to.

**He is the cleanest man in the dirtiest room.** A seedy tavern with a seedy
landlord is one note played twice; a seedy tavern with a fastidious one is a
room somebody is holding together and you can see what it is costing him. He
knows everything about everybody because people tell a bartender, and he will
not tell you a word of it, which makes him the only person in town who is safe.

And the hook, which costs one sentence: **he remembers your drink.** Not you —
your drink. He cannot say why he already knows it and does not enjoy being
asked. Spite is the only person who half-remembers the loop. Tobin is the first
evidence that it leaks into people who do not.

He is also the game's **first working trade**: a drink is `+16 grit` and
`deadline -1`, which is a real cost and a real benefit in the vocabulary
`Objectives.apply_effects` already implements. The second one is worse, because
a bar you can stand at forever is not a bar.

### Ansel Cobb — `cobb`

`BUILDINGS.md` #2 is four words: *General Store (owner sleeps in back)*. The four
words are the character — he has staff (#8 works here) and still sleeps in the
shop, because he cannot bear to leave the stock and because the shop is the only
place he has ever been any use.

**His mechanic is his joke: the shop is slow.** He is delighted by a customer
and constitutionally incapable of completing a sale, because every item has a
story and you are going to hear it (the shelf costs you an hour and eleven
minutes of rope).

There is no economy yet, so this is a conversation with the *shape* of a shop in
it, and the part worth keeping when somebody builds the real one is the
**order**: you ask for something, he writes it in the ledger, and it is under the
counter with your name on it next time. Browse → buy → collect is a shop's real
loop and it is three tags. It is also the second-meeting content.

## 4. The portraits, and who earned one

**The children and Mrs Ollard.** The children because the change of heart is the
first person in the game the player talks round and the beat deserves a face;
Mrs Ollard because half of what she is only exists in an expression. Bram, Tobin
and Cobb get a sprite and `DialogueScreen`'s lettered plate, which is a decision
and not a shortfall — four more faces at this quality is another pass, and a
mediocre face is worse than an initial in a box.

`pp_face()` is one head and the moods are a delta dictionary over it, so
`posted` and `stood_down` cannot be two different children in exactly the way
`kind` and `wary` cannot be two different men. **The light does not change
between moods**, for the reason Spite's doc gives: an early cut of `wary` dimmed
a step and bought a corpse rather than a mood.

Two techniques Spite's two faces did not need:

**The gaze.** Moving a pupil one pixel inside an otherwise unchanged eye is the
cheapest expression in the toolkit and the only one that draws *not here*. Mrs
Ollard's `adrift` is a gaze delta and almost nothing else — same eyes, open the
same amount, looking a pixel past you.

**The prop as the mood.** The children's `posted` has the stick across the
bottom of the plate; `stood_down` does not. The gate is a literal object in the
picture and taking it away is the whole beat, and it reads at 1:1 before either
face resolves. **Where a mood is about a decision rather than a feeling, move the
object.**

Measured: `nosy` vs `adrift` differ in 14.1% of plate pixels, `posted` vs
`stood_down` in 17.8%, against Spite's 20.2%. Both at 56 logical pixels doubled
to the 112 `DialogueScreen._portrait()` reserves.

## 5. The gate: how it is wired, and the one thing still missing

The branch in `fort_children` is **two replies with the same words and opposite
`when` clauses**, so the player never sees the mechanism — only one of them is
ever on screen.

They read the run tag `town_quiet`, which `data/objectives/town.json` grants in
the `close_town_anomalies` reward and which `data/content/interactables.json`
also uses for `leaf_gate`'s `blocks_until_tag`. That works, and it works in the
run you finish the town in.

**It does not survive the loop.** A run tag dies with the run; the objective does
not, and not dying is the entire point of that objective. So on the next loop
the children refuse again although the town is provably finished. The fix is
four lines in `scripts/autoload/Rules.gd`:

```gdscript
"objective_done":
    if not Objectives.is_complete(String(want)):
        return false
```

plus `"objective_done"` in `data/schema.json` → `vocabulary.when_keys`
(`validate_data.py` reconciles that list against `Rules.passes` and will fail if
you add one without the other), and then the two `when` dictionaries in
`data/dialogue/fort.json` become `{ "objective_done": "close_town_anomalies" }`
and its negation. **This is the highest-value follow-up in this whole change.**

`has_tag` and `lacks_tag` are the *only* conditions a conversation can currently
evaluate at all: `Dialogue.context()` returns `run.ctx()`, which carries `zone`,
`area`, `trinkets` and `tags` and nothing else, so `tier_gte`, `phase`, `region`,
`kind`, `axis`, `has_relic` and `hp_below` all silently pass or silently fail in
dialogue. Worth knowing before writing the next conversation.

### The schema seam that made moods possible

`dialogue_nodes.refs.mood` used to point at `spite_alters`, so **only Spite could
have a mood at all** — any other speaker's mood id was a validation error. Fixed
by giving `spite_alters` `"id_space": "moods"` and adding a `speaker_moods` type
(`speakers[].moods[]`) in the same space. Two ref strings changed and both now
point at a superset, so nothing that worked before behaves differently.

A speaker with no `moods` and no `mood` on its nodes resolves to
`assets/portraits/<stem>.png`, which does not exist, and gets the lettered
plate — which is how Bram, Tobin and Cobb work.

**Nothing still validates that a mood has a portrait file.** Rename a mood and
the art silently stops resolving and no test fails. The schema now knows the
mood ids, so this is a much cheaper win than it was: one loop in
`tools/validate_data.py` over `speaker_moods` checking
`assets/portraits/<portrait>_<id>.png` exists, exactly the way `critter_pass()`
already checks sprite sheets.

## 6. What is not built

Honest list, in the order somebody should pick them up.

- **`objective_done`.** §5. Without it the gate reopens for one run only.
- **The candy buff.** Kevin's notes want a temporary speed buff and a medication
  debuff. A dialogue effect cannot grant a buff — `Objectives.apply_effects`
  implements `grit`, `deadline`, `resolve`, `item`, `tag`, `objective`, `log`,
  and `buff` lives in the *hook* vocabulary that interactables use. So the sweet
  pays grit, which is real. The buff version wants either a `buff` arm in
  `Objectives.apply_effects` or an interactable on her carrying
  `{ "type": "buff", "buff": "candy" }` plus two records in
  `data/content/buffs.json`.
  The *randomness* is deliberately not built and should stay that way: a
  conversation cannot roll dice, and faking it teaches the player the answer in
  one try. The first sweet in a loop is a sweet and the second is the other tin,
  because she has forgotten — which is not a dice roll, it is her.
- **The cat quest.** The nose is built and it is data:
  `data/content/critters.json` now bolts the cat from `carrying_candy` and lures
  it with `carrying_fish`, candy first because order is priority. Missing: the
  two items, whatever sets `cat_home`, and the line-of-sight clause — *"it cannot
  have line of sight, you must use buildings to make it a surprise"* — which
  needs a term `HJCritters._conditions_met` does not have. The payoff branch and
  the secret name are written and wait on `cat_home`.
- **The mind palace character card.** The notes want the secret name remembered
  there. There is no character card, so `knows_secret_name` is a run tag and the
  knowledge dies with the loop, which is at least thematically the correct kind
  of sad.
- **The shop.** No inventory screen, no buy, no sell. Cobb's `order` branch and
  Bram's `barter` branch are shaped so a real one drops in where they are.
- **Placement.** Not this work's business and deliberately so. The world agent
  has already placed `leaf_gate` → `fort_children`; the other five want an
  interactables catalogue entry each with a `Talk` verb, a `critter` naming the
  species, and a `{"type": "dialogue"}` effect. Ids in §7.
- **Ten more townspeople.** #77 wants fifteen. The base is built and each of
  them is now about twenty lines. The constraint that will bite first is
  **crowns**: eight shapes are used and the ninth needs inventing, not reusing.

Look at these rather than at the PNGs.
