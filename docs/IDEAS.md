# Ideas not yet built

Kevin's list, captured verbatim in substance and grouped. **Nothing here is
implemented.** It is here so it is not lost, and so that when something *is*
built the thinking behind it has somewhere to live.

Order below is grouping, not priority.

## Getting around

- **Fast travel at a step cost.** Think Pokémon travel items rather than a menu
  teleport — a consumable you carry, so the decision is "do I spend this here".
  Fits the step economy directly: distance is already the currency.
- **Hunter / finder items.** Something that tells you where the interesting
  thing is, rather than walking the whole ring to find it.

## The town as an economy

- **Resource gathering and selling.** Gives the outer rings a reason to exist
  beyond difficulty, and gives walking a second output besides progress.
- **Townspeople with scripted paths and jobs.** The single biggest "the world is
  alive" lever — a person who is somewhere different at a different hour does
  more than any amount of scenery.
- **Quests**: delivery, fetch, find, gather. Note these are four different
  shapes, not four skins of one shape: delivery is a route, fetch is a
  round trip, find is search, gather is volume.

## Carrying things

- **Collectables.**
- **Temporal pouch** — a bag upgrade specifically for them, so collecting has a
  ceiling you can raise.

## Things in the world

- **Mini bosses.**
- **Mobs**, with a strong through-line: most of these attack *the loop* rather
  than the body, which is the right instinct for a game with no combat.
  - **Time wraiths** — teleport you into some time dungeon.
  - **Neural worms** — take control of you for X steps. (Steps are the
    currency, so this is theft of exactly the thing that matters.)
  - **Energy vampires** — drain Grit.
  - **Temporal trash grubs** — squishable, and they *eat anomalies*, so they are
    a threat to your content rather than to you.
  - **Regular animals** — non-interactable, with maybe rare exceptions.
  - **Phantoms** — inhabit regular animals and people, which is what makes the
    non-interactable animals worth having: any of them might not be one.
  - **Forest troll** — punts you X distance if it catches you; stone during
    daytime. A hazard that is also a clock.

## Notes worth keeping

The mob list is unusually coherent. Nothing there does damage; they take steps,
Grit, position, control, or content. That is consistent with a game whose only
real resources are time, distance and whether you did the thing — and it means
none of them need a combat system to be threatening.

"Stone during daytime" and "teleport you to a time dungeon" both imply the world
has a clock the player can read and plan around. There isn't one yet beyond the
deadline; several of these ideas want one.
