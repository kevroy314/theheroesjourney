The way multiplayer works in a game like this may seem non-obvious at first. Players are unlikely to be playing simultaneously and none of the game mechanics require two players as designed. The story is also very driven on a one-to-one relationship between the antogonistic (future version of the player) and the player. However, even though the player is living in their own pre-destination paradox, other players are as well. The variations on each run are a reflection of the multi-verse bleed overs that are occuring in adjacent timelines that the player ends up inhabiting or not. So this is our narrative route to bringing in other players.

First principle: connecting player worlds is not free. If you want to join with another player, you have to build a room in the mind palace called the Theater of the Mind. This room produces a share key that allows two players to link, but that connection costs resolve for both players. The benefit is they get a temporary buff "Not Alone" that provides a grit multiplier and increased Resolve gain for any social task for the rest of the run. It also makes a ghost copy of the other player in their world that cannot interact with anything in their world but is visible in real time. It also enables a chat feature which allows players to DM or group chat. Every time a new player is added, it's always one-to-one by default, however if a group of people all want to be visible, a convenience feature called The Ritual can be accessed in the Theater of the Mind which is basically a share link that invites anyone. However, it is one time (24 hours up to 1 week) and has a fixed cost for all players involved that is more expensive than a one-to-one (but cheaper than doing all combinations of one-to-ones to otherwise form the same connection). Player connections can be dropped at any time, but re-linking with another player is permanently free and does not impart a new buff on reconnect.

This is a mid-game mechanic. Players who have not unlocked the Theater of the Mind cannot join.

Other benefits of multiplayer are Challenges. 

Players may walk up to each others ghosts and see a stat card at any time. The stat card contains the basic information about the player's current step count, current grit, number of loops completed, and number of each task type completed successfully/failed (or downgraded). Challenges are initiated by players clicking a small popover "Challenge" button that appears over other players. Group challenges require a special item called the Transdimensional Towel (i.e. "throw down the towel") that can only be bought at a late game merchant. When the towel is thrown down, any players may click it to participate. Otherwise one-to-one challenges can be initiated just by proximate contact. 

Challenges come in two flavors: competative and cooperative. Competative challenges cannot be refused and are inherently adversarial. Throwing down a towel for a competative challenge is strictly consenual as no one can force you to click the towel. In a competative challenge, the challenging user selects 1 task they wish to challenge the other user to. This drops a "shadow ghost" version of the player so when the defending player comes back, they know they've been challenged. They must resolve the challenge before they can continue moving. The defender selects 2 more task options of their chosing, and a RNG event occurs that "slot machines" through the 3 options, selecting one at random. Each task in the selection has a wager associated with it. This may be steps or grit. Challenges are parallel anomalies that run independently of the normal anomaly screens and have their own independent timer. The victory condition of a challenge varies depending on the challenge. One example challenge would be a step challenge - whichever player accumulates the most measured steps (i.e. not steps as a result of multipliers, buffs, or anything else, just raw sensor steps) within the challenge window wins. Challenge windows are always 24 hours, 48 hours, or 72 hours. Other examples of challenge types are anomaly challenges (complete the most anomalies in unit time), grit challenges (accumulate the most grit in unit time). Other challenge ideas will come later depending on other mechanics. 

Challenges auto-resolve at the end of the timer based on the in game measurables. Users are notified of the resolution when they log back in.

Cooperative challenges involve a similar structure to competative but the group is aiming for a target (sum of steps, sum of grid, sum of anomalies, etc). The wagers are positive/negative in this case where if the group loses, everyone loses the wager, if the group wins, everyone wins it.

Multiple challenges may be active at once up to a limit determined by the level of the lowest player level Theater of the Mind. Theater of the Mind is upgradable up to 5 simultaneous independent challenges. Critically, challenges can never be initiated remotely (i.e. without player proximity) other than via the towel (in which case all players must still touch the towel). The towel cannot be picked up once thrown, but if no one uses it, it may be picked back up for 24 hours after it times out (with a 72 hour time out).

Challenges may also be races (get from the challenge start to some location in fastest wall clock time or fewest steps), mob events, or other fun things related to future mechanics.
