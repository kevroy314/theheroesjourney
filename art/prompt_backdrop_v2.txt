THE LIGHTING — this is the important part, and the previous version got it wrong by being too dark.

1. There is now a PRACTICAL LIGHT SOURCE INSIDE THE ROOM: a small oil lamp with a low flame, sitting on the floor beside the bed, near the bottom left. It is dim and warm. It throws a soft pool of light across the floorboards and up the near edge of the bed frame.
2. Because of that lamp, THE FLOOR IS CLEARLY VISIBLE. The floorboards should sit at roughly 20 percent brightness — dark, but you can plainly read the boards, their seams, and the grain. They must NOT be near-black. Nothing in the lower half of the picture should be crushed to pure shadow.
3. The window is still the brightest area of the picture.
4. The one amber light on the mountain is still the most SATURATED point in the picture. The lamp is warm but muted and desaturated by comparison — the mountain light stays the thing your eye goes to.
5. Overall the room should read as "dimly lit at dawn", not "unlit". A phone screen in daylight must still show the floor and the bed.

COMPOSITION — this also changes.
- Put the window HIGH: its top edge should be cropped by the top edge of the image, and the whole window, the mountain and its light should sit entirely within the TOP THIRD of the picture.
- The LOWER TWO THIRDS is floor, bed and door — and it must be a QUIET BAND: even, low contrast, softly lit by the lamp, with no fine detail, no bright highlights and no busy texture. Interface panels will be laid over this area and must stay readable, so keep it calm and let it read as one continuous surface.
